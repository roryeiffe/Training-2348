## Before the Interview
1. Go through the topics on the curriculum. For each topic, ask
  - What is it?
  - Why is it useful?
  - How is it used?
2. Practice answering questions either by yourself or with a teammate/friend. It also helps to practice being the interviewer as it will clue you in on what things to look out for. 
  - If no one is available, you can also leverage AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.) to run mock interviews with. As with all AI use, make sure to verify important information. 
3. Work on projects or practical challenges to reinforce topics and provide you with examples to reference during the interview.

## During the Interview
1. Come up with an answer structure ahead of time. For example (1. Answer the question. 2. Give more details. 3. Provide an example if applicable. 4. Summarize)
2. Keep a glass of water nearby. You can take a sip to buy time if you need to think for a second. Just try not to pause for too long.
3. Use technical terminology, when applicable.
4. Even if you don't know the answer, try to answer what you do know. Worst case, put a positive spin on your answer ("I don't remember that right now but I will look into it.")
5. If you want to write something down, it's always good to check with the interviewer first

## After the Interview
1. Remember what questions/topics gave you trouble and focus studying on those.

## Soft Skills Examples
- Being centered in frame
- Not talking in monotone
  - Avoid mumbling
- Eye Contact - look at camera or slightly above
- Avoid distractions like eating
- Background - can use virtual background
  - blur background
  - If not blurred, just make sure it's free of clutter
- Have questions prepared to ask the interviewer?

## Technical Skills
- Technical Terminology
- Long answers (at least 4-5 sentences total)
  - Answer
  - Example

    - Could also be from some non-project code or a theoretical example
  - Explain/Conclude
